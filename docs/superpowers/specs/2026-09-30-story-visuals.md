# Story visuals: per-chart designs

Output of the visualization research workflow on 2026-09-30.
- 5 parallel catalog sweeps found 121 visualization types.
- A matcher assigned them to chapters.
- An honesty critic and a founder-readability critic reviewed the result, and a final revision followed.

Where a design names data we do not have, the data contract in the master plan wins:
- the labeling-error interval layer is dropped;
- partial pooling across quarters is replaced by the bootstrap and certainty fade;
- Leiden clustering is replaced by scipy average linkage.

## Signature visuals

- Zooming unit field: 3.99M comments shrink to 70.6k firsthand problems with a visible 1,000-to-100 rescale, then the same survivor dots recolor to show who is speaking (stated role, named country)
- Voronoi treemap of needs: 13 groups and 150 cards in one circle, with the 42% unplaced shown as a grey sector so the taxonomy's blind spot is part of the hero
- Coping triangle built from three bars: one need's pay, hack and quit rates collapse into its dot, then every need lands between 'They pay', 'They hack' and 'They quit', faded where evidence is thin
- Builders vs complainers scatter: 150 needs on log-log axes against a 'launches match pain' diagonal, with a shaded underbuilt zone
- Weighted opportunity explorer: founder-set weights re-rank the needs live, with contribution bars showing why and rank quantile dots showing how stable each rank is

## Global (all chapters): Reading system: one interval glyph, one certainty fade, 6-hue palette, bottom detail sheet

- **Question:** How does a reader learn one way to read size, color and uncertainty, and keep trusting it for 25 charts?
- **Why this form:** Both critiques found the same failure: five uncertainty encodings, mixed gates (q < 0.05, q < 0.10, 80%, 95%), 13 hues and share reused as a size cue everywhere. One grammar, taught once and reused everywhere, is what lets novel forms stay readable.
- **Data mapping:** Interval glyph (used for every CI): dot = estimate, thick core = 50% interval, thin bar = 95% interval, and an outer faint bar = 95% interval plus labeling error on headline shares (forest plot, complaint rate, builders ratio, Top 10). All intervals come from a thread-cluster bootstrap with 1,000 joint replicates (the same resampled threads across every card, month and metric). Wilson intervals are dropped. Certainty fade (replaces VSUP, hatching and grey-or-color gates): fill opacity has 3 steps set by the relative 95% half-width (under 25% of the estimate = full, 25-50% = 60%, over 50% = 30%). One significance convention for the whole essay: BH q < 0.05 with 95% intervals, printed once in a persistent legend chip. The only other encoding is rank quantile dots in chapter 6. Color: 6 super-group hues (light and dark variants for the 13 groups) plus neutral grey, direct labels at the point of use, and highlight mode in busy charts (only the selected or annotated group is colored). Size: share is encoded by area only where area is the primary quantity (Voronoi, domain bubbles, waffles), and each of those sits next to a length-based companion. Everywhere else marks are fixed size, or sized by n when precision is the point. Months use real names (for example Oct 2025 to Sep 2026), never P01-P12.
- **Interaction:** A 'How to read the bars' explainer (animated: 1 glyph, 3 labels) at its first use in chapter 1, and a legend chip in the sticky header that reopens it. On every chart, tapping a mark opens one sticky bottom detail sheet with previous and next buttons, so readers can step through items without precise taps. Hit areas are at least 44px, built from invisible Delaunay/Voronoi touch targets. Every chart has a 'table' toggle, a screen-reader summary sentence, and a CSV of its aggregates. A skip-nav mode shows one chart per chapter (the executive path). Each chapter ends with one bold 'number to remember'.
- **Uncertainty:** Every caption states 'inner bars: sampling uncertainty (resampled by thread); outer bar: plus labeling error'. No chart switches color on a p-value. Where a chart must mark significance, it uses the single q < 0.05 convention in text.
- **Mobile:** The same glyph at 375px (core 4px, bar 1.5px). Bottom sheet replaces all tooltips. Reduced-motion path swaps animations for crossfades or stacked static states and is tested at 375px. Performance budget: each chapter's libraries and JSON lazy-load through IntersectionObserver, first screen under 250 KB gzipped, canvas for any chart with more than 1,000 marks.
- **Static X version:** Every static export carries a 200px legend tile bottom-right (glyph, fade steps, 'q < 0.05, 95% intervals, resampled by thread') and the source line 'Hacker News comments, Oct 2025 to Sep 2026, aggregates only'.
- **Library:** Shared module on D3 v7 + Observable Plot (jsDelivr). Palette checked with the dataviz skill validator and a CVD simulator at build time.

## Global (all chapters): Data contract and build-time gates (not a chart)

- **Question:** What must the pipeline produce before any chart can ship honestly?
- **Why this form:** Most critique issues are data issues that no visual encoding can fix. Listing them as build gates means a chart that lacks its inputs renders a placeholder instead of an overconfident picture.
- **Data mapping:** Blocking inputs: (1) thread-cluster bootstrap, 1,000 joint replicates with a saved replicate index, plus a thread-and-author two-way run as a sensitivity check and the design effect per card. (2) Whether the 605k screened comments are a random sample of the 3.68M or the output of a prefilter. (3) Per-stage precision and recall from the human audit (n, class balance), split by domain and group where n allows, feeding a prediction-powered or Rogan-Gladen correction of headline shares. (4) Whether card, domain and role labels are single or multi-label per problem; a primary card per problem if multi-label. (5) Absolute rates: problems per 10k eligible comments by card, group and quarter. (6) Distinct authors and distinct threads per card, top-1% author share, and a one-vote-per-author rerun. (7) Confirmation that one domain labeler ran on all 3.99M comments and on the problems. (8) Show HN sample n, period, sampling method, match rate and an audited match precision. (9) A one-sentence definition and inclusion rule for each of the 150 cards. (10) Any spend or price magnitude behind 'paid', or an explicit 'flag only'. (11) Thread age at data cutoff, and how deleted, duplicate and bot comments were handled. (12) How the 0-2 similarity score was produced and any human check. (13) Term filter: k-anonymity (20 comments, 10 authors) plus a denylist for person names, handles, URLs and tool names, and a human pass over bigrams for quasi-quotes. (14) Correlation matrix of the opportunity metrics with each other and with card size.
- **Interaction:** None for readers. Each gate is a check in the build script. A failed gate swaps the affected component for a 'not measured' or 'too few mentions to show' placeholder and logs it.
- **Uncertainty:** This section is where uncertainty is produced. Every JSON interval field records its method (thread bootstrap, labeling-error adjusted or not) so the glyph can draw the right layers.
- **Mobile:** Not applicable.
- **Static X version:** Not exported.
- **Library:** Python pipeline (pandas, scipy, leidenalg) writing versioned JSON; a JSON-schema check runs before the static build.

## Global (all chapters): 'Find your need' mind map drawer with follow-this-card mode

- **Question:** How does a founder find their corner of the map and keep it in view through the whole essay?
- **Why this form:** The user asked for mind mapping. As a chapter chart it repeated the Voronoi hierarchy and its leaf sizes conflicted with it. As a persistent navigator it does the job a mind map is good at: showing structure and letting people jump. Following one card through every chart turns the essay into a personal read.
- **Data mapping:** Root 'Firsthand problems' -> 13 group nodes -> 150 card leaves. Structure only: leaves are equal-size dots colored by super-group, and each label carries its full-year share as an inline number ('Invoice chasing 1.4%'). The legend states that arc span shows how many cards a group has, not its size. Hovering or tapping a leaf shows the card's one-sentence definition and inclusion rule.
- **Interaction:** Opens from a button in the sticky header. Groups start collapsed, and there is a search box. Selecting a leaf sets the global followed card (stored in the URL hash). Every later chart outlines and labels that card even when it would otherwise be grey, and a sticky chip shows its name with 'open profile' and 'stop following'.
- **Uncertainty:** None drawn (navigation). The share number links to the card's interval in the bottom sheet.
- **Mobile:** An indented outline list with inline share numbers and one group open at a time, in a full-height drawer.
- **Static X version:** Fully expanded radial tree at 1600x900, group labels bold, the top 3 cards per group labeled with shares. Used as the essay's table-of-contents image.
- **Library:** D3 v7 (d3.tree, radial layout) from jsDelivr

## 0. Raw material: Zooming unit field with visible rescale and a 'who is speaking' recolor

- **Question:** How do 3.99M comments become 70.6k firsthand problems and about 41k placed on 150 needs, and who is speaking?
- **Why this form:** The funnel spans two orders of magnitude. A countable dot field keeps each stage area-true, and the zoom shows the survivors without a log scale. Recoloring the same survivor dots by stated role and named country shows HN's population bias at the start of the essay without adding a chart.
- **Data mapping:** Step 1: 3,990 dots (1 dot = 1,000 comments), with a 12-bar column chart of comments per month inside the step (bars primary, a mean line, partial months hatched and noted). Step 2: 3,680 eligible lit, 310 removed by rule in texture A. Step 3: 605 screened. If the 605k is a prefilter output, the other 3,075 dots take texture B 'filtered out before the classifier'. If it is a random sample, they take a dotted outline 'not examined', and a side label gives the estimated firsthand problems in the full corpus (about 430k) with a thread-bootstrap interval. Step 4: 71 firsthand dots lit. Each splits into a 10-dot mini-grid over 600ms, a large unit badge animates from '1 dot = 1,000' to '1 dot = 100', and a 1:10 inset outline stays drawn around the zoomed region (706 dots). Step 5: 410 placed on a card (ink), 296 'not placed on any card' (texture C). Step 6: the 706 dots recolor to 'states a role' vs not, then role mix among those who state one. Step 7: 'names a country' vs not, then US vs rest. Retention labels beside steps: 92%, 16%, 12%, 58%.
- **Interaction:** Scrollama sticky graphic, 7 steps. Tap a stage label to highlight its dots. A 'show as numbers' toggle swaps in a drop-off table with every count and retention rate.
- **Uncertainty:** Counts are exact. The corpus-wide estimate (if the 605k is a sample) carries the interval glyph. The step 5 caption separates two numbers: '58% of problems were placed on a card (exact)' and 'X% of placements were correct (audit, n = ..., 95% interval)'. The step 6-7 caption reads 'HN skews toward developers and the US; every later share means share of what this crowd reports'.
- **Mobile:** 4px cells, 50 columns (200x320px), so step text fits under the graphic. With reduced motion: stacked static panels, each with its unit badge in large type.
- **Static X version:** Four panels at 1600x900: full field, eligible, screened (with texture legend), zoomed firsthand field with placed dots lit and the unit badge in each. Title: '3.99M comments. 70.6k firsthand problems. 150 needs.'
- **Library:** D3 v7 (canvas) + Scrollama, from jsDelivr

## 2. Loud vs quiet: Morphing domain bubbles that settle into a complaint-rate lollipop

- **Question:** Which domains dominate talk but produce little pain, and which are quiet but full of it?
- **Why this form:** Object constancy is the argument: each domain grows or shrinks in place as the measure switches from talk to pain, then the same circles slide into sorted rows where length and a CI give the precise reading. One object carries both the memorable moment and the exact answer.
- **Data mapping:** Prerequisite: one domain labeler for all 3.99M comments and the problems, stated in the caption (if not, this chart is replaced by the lollipop alone with a confound warning). State A: 20 circles, area = share of all comments. State B: area = share of firsthand problems. Positions computed once from each domain's larger radius, so nothing drifts. Share numbers printed inside or beside each circle. Color = above or below the overall complaint rate, with the certainty fade. State C: circles slide into 20 rows, x = complaint rate (firsthand problems per 1,000 comments in the domain) on a log axis, with a reference line at the overall rate, sorted by rate, interval glyph including the labeling-error layer. Badge: 'x2.4 more pain than talk' (domain rate over overall rate).
- **Interaction:** Three scroll steps (A, B, C) or a tap toggle. Tap a bubble or row for both shares, the rate and its interval. Labels follow story value: top 3 each direction plus the top 2 by size.
- **Uncertainty:** Interval glyph on the rate (sampling plus labeling error, since recall may differ by domain). Certainty fade on bubble fill. Caption: 'inner bar: resampled by thread; outer: plus classifier error by domain'.
- **Mobile:** A 343px square for states A and B with a tap toggle, then the lollipop with labels above rows. Reduced motion: crossfade between three static states.
- **Static X version:** The 20-row lollipop fills the left two-thirds at 1600x900; two small bubble clusters ('What HN talks about', 'What HN complains about') sit on the right, with the 3 biggest movers badged.

## 2. Loud vs quiet: Breadth beeswarm with an expandable residual card-by-domain matrix

- **Question:** Which needs are cross-domain platform plays, and which live inside one domain (Dig deeper)?
- **Why this form:** The founder question is breadth, so a single-axis beeswarm of breadth answers it on any screen. The 3,000-cell matrix stays for desktop explorers, rebuilt on shrunken residuals so small cards do not look confident and blocks must beat shuffled data.
- **Data mapping:** Beeswarm (visible above the expander): 150 cards, x = bias-corrected domain entropy (Chao-Shen, or rarefied to a common n), fixed dot size, super-group color in highlight mode, the 5 broadest labeled.
